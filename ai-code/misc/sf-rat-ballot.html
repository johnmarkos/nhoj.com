SF Rat Ballot

A stylized Bayesian-rationalist ballot guide for San Francisco's June 2, 2026 election: high expected value, state capacity, abundance, institutional competence, clean incentives, and explicit uncertainty.

Not official: this is not affiliated with Eliezer Yudkowsky, any campaign, or any rationalist organization. It is a POC written in the style of a risk-aware, Bayesian voter. Confidence means "how likely this is the best choice under these priors," not the chance of winning.
Statewide Local Offices District Races Measures
Model priors

Prefer boring competence over vibes. Prefer mechanisms that improve incentives over symbolic punishment. Discount claims that require a miracle budget or perfect implementation. Weight housing abundance, institutional reliability, disaster preparedness, and AI/technology governance unusually heavily. Treat low-salience offices as jobs with skill requirements, not personality contests.

Local Measures

Proposition A

Earthquake Safety and Emergency Response Bond
72%
Rat pick
Yes

High-tail-risk city resilience is exactly the kind of thing voters underfund. The bond has implementation and debt risks, but emergency water, seismically safe response facilities, and disaster logistics have positive expected value in San Francisco.

Proposition B

Lifetime term limits for Mayor and Supervisors
65%
Rat pick
No

Term limits are a blunt anti-incumbent heuristic. Lifetime bans reduce voter choice, destroy institutional memory, and shift power toward staff, lobbyists, and interest groups. If someone is bad, beat them.

Proposition C

Decreases to business taxes
60%
Rat pick
Yes

Not perfect, but the model likes reducing tax cliffs and making San Francisco less hostile to business formation. It also strategically blocks the worse Prop D regime if C receives more votes.

Proposition D

Higher top-executive-pay business tax
72%
Rat pick
No

The revenue need is real, but this mechanism is too punitive, narrow, and relocation-sensitive. Taxing CEO-worker pay ratios is emotionally satisfying and economically weird; the model prefers broader, simpler revenue tools.

Because C and D conflict, the model votes Yes on C and No on D.
